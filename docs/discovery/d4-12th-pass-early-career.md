# Who hires a 10th-pass / 12th-pass / ITI candidate in India

Discovery note, 27 September 2026. Persona: just passed Class 12, or 10th-pass / ITI / diploma
holder, 17-20, looking for a first job or a trade licence. Searched the way that candidate searches:
`railway group d`, `constable bharti`, `army agniveer`, `ssc gd`, `state police constable`,
`ITI vacancy`, `driver recruitment exam`, and the Hinglish versions of the same.

This is the fan-out map. The exam records under `content/exams/d4-*.md` are the leaf nodes. What
follows is the thing a candidate actually needs and no exam portal gives them: for each state, which
board runs which test, and the one URL where the notice lands.

**The organising fact.** For this persona there is no national exam. There is a national *floor* —
SSC GD Constable, RRB Group D Level-1, Agniveer (GD) — and then roughly thirty independent state
police recruitment boards, each with its own notification, its own physical standards, its own
application portal and its own calendar. They do not synchronise. In 2026 the Andhra Pradesh and
Telangana constable notices landed eleven days apart, in the same fortnight, with completely
different physical standards. A candidate who waits for "the" constable exam misses both.

until the next cycle opens, and the state boards below are the only doors open right now.

## What the fan-out teaches a student

1. **The national tests and the state tests do not overlap in time.** The national written papers for
   this persona all fell between December and April in 2026. The state constable notices fall between
   June and September. A candidate who prepares only for SSC GD is idle exactly when the state boards
   are open.
2. **Applications close long before the examination.** Karnataka's window was eleven days. Odisha's
   was eleven days. Telangana's had to be extended by a week. And the exam itself is then months away
   and runs over a window, not a date.
3. **The trade posts are a separate application.** Telangana's constable mechanic and driver posts
   have no written paper at all. Andhra issues a separate notification for them. A candidate with an ITI
   certificate is often looking at the wrong notice.
4. **The physical standards decide the outcome, not the marks.** Andhra measures men at 167.6 cm with
   86.3 cm chest, Ladakh at 162 cm with 81/85 cm, Bihar's Special Branch at 165 cm or 160 cm by
   category, Punjab runs a PST and PMT with specified parameters, and Telangana's PMT uses digital
   measurement. Madhya Pradesh is unusual in *marking* the physical events (800 m, long jump, shot put
   at 40/30/30) rather than only gating on them. HPPSC awards height-band marks.
5. **Some states gate on a qualifying test taken earlier.** Haryana's whole police and Group D chain
   runs through HSSC CET. Rajasthan's runs through the RSSB CET at Senior Secondary level. A candidate
   with no CET score is locked out of posts they can see.
6. **The domiciliary states are not for everyone.** Ladakh requires a Ladakh domicile certificate
   outright, including residence in an eligible border village for the Border Battalion. Punjab does
   not post a selected constable in the home district. Most states treat an out-of-state applicant as
   unreserved, which means the reservation arithmetic works against them.
7. **Read the extension notice, not just the original.** Himachal extended to 21 August 2026 by a
   separate notice. Telangana extended to 16 September 2026 by a separate press note. The original
   deadline in the advertisement is the one most portals will still show you.
